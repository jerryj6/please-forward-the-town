# Design Research Dataroom 7 — Traffic-as-Emergent-Villain

Seeded sources: Mini Motorways' entropy pacing (Bach/Asquith —
congestion emerges from your own past choices, not a scripted enemy),
queue-theory in puzzle economies, and PFT's shared-infrastructure
congestion. Mapped concretely.

## 1. The villain is the player's own history

- **Source:** Mini Motorways' design docs — there is no enemy; the
  difficulty is generated by earlier layout decisions compounding.
  Entropy is authored by the player, so failure reads as "I caused
  this" — motivation to retry, not blame.
- **PFT application:** every strand event is player-authored: the
  bridge you packed, the sign you pocketed, the ferry you sold. The
  `recovery` taxonomy is literally the "how badly did I compound"
  readout — `redeploy` = small debt, `undo` = structural debt,
  `none` = bankruptcy. Congestion as emergent villain, named.

## 2. Queues form at shared infrastructure, not entities

- **Source:** Motorways' congestion happens at intersections —
  shared edges, not the cars. The game's skill is edge-management.
- **PFT application:** our congestion lives at the same place —
  shared edges (bridge sockets, ferry, postal links). Enumeration
  proves the queuing is the whole game: the DENY table lists ONLY
  infrastructure verbs; the coop split on pft-12 shows the real
  contention is "who needs the crossing when" — a scheduling
  problem on shared edges.

## 3. Entropy must be controllable, not just visible

- **Source:** Motorways gives the player entropy-management tools
  (traffic lights, motorways) at the same rate entropy grows —
  escalation + countermeasures in lockstep.
- **PFT application:** PFT's countermeasures scale with congestion:
  `undo` (free), `redeploy` (one move), postal routing (bypass the
  water entirely). The move-slack bands (20–24 on pft-07, 44–46 on
  pft-12) ARE the player's entropy budget — and the minimalism
  probes show the verified plans stay inside it.

## 4. Late-game entropy should peak, then resolve

- **Source:** Motorways' endgame is congestion peaking past capacity —
  the last minutes are the densest. Good entropy pacing peaks, then
  either resolves (win) or visibly overwhelms (lose).
- **PFT application:** the finale encodes this exactly: pft-12's
  endgame has the highest decision density in the campaign (every
  piece + every exit converging on two crossings) — the seeded-window
  enum shows 23 completions in the final 8 moves, the widest branch
  point of any level. Entropy peaks at the last crossing, then the
  office deploy resolves it.

## 5. Show the congestion source in the verdict

- **Source:** Motorways' fail readout is the screen itself — you can
  SEE the jammed intersection when it ends. Readable villains produce
  readable losses.
- **PFT application:** strand events carry the congestion source as
  data ("Courier Lark cannot reach North Dock — recoverable: redeploy
  the still-owned piece") — the reason IS the jammed intersection.
  The post-accept probe this pass keeps the record honest: a stale
  verdict is marked superseded rather than silently wrong — readable
  villains need honest scoreboards.
